What a shift schedule really is

the representation behind the linehaul demo · 7 delivery centres · 2 sorting hubs · 7 trucks · all data fictional
← evolution demo

The schedule on the main demo looks like hundreds of hand-placed blocks. It isn't. Every block is forced: the moment you say which DC this truck loads at next, the network's rules — drive, queue for the dock, load, run to the sooner-free sorter, queue, unload — fix every timestamp that follows. So a whole shift schedule is just a list of DC choices, and a dispatch policy is the short program that generates them. That is the thing AlphaEvolve evolves.

Step 1 · the atomdrag the dock clocks — the block redraws

One decision produces one block of the timetable

A truck has just finished unloading and is standing at a sorting hub. Pick the DC it goes to next. Nothing else is chosen by anyone: the drive is distance ÷ speed, the queue is however long the dock is already booked for, the load is capacity ÷ belt rate, and the truck tips at whichever sorter can take it soonest. This is the entire vocabulary a schedule is written in.

    Step 5 · why this representationa timetable answers one day; a policy answers any day
    
    
    Why evolve the rule and not the timetable
    
    
    A schedule is only valid for the network it was drawn on. Move a depot, slow a belt, swap a
    trailer, and every timestamp after the change is wrong. So the evaluator never scores a timetable — it scores a
    rule, by re-running it on three different networks and averaging. The rule has to re-derive the
    schedule each time, which is exactly what a dispatcher does at 6am on a real Tuesday.
    
    
    
      
    
        
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
      
          
    • 1A timetable can't be scored fairly. It would ace the network it was drawn for and fall apart on the other two. Averaging over three perturbed networks makes overfitting cost points.
      • 
          
    • 2The search space is the right size. A shift has hundreds of decisions; a policy has a few dozen lines. Gemini can rewrite lines. Nobody can search 10168 timetables.
      • 
          
    • 3The output is deployable. What comes out is a dispatch rule an operations team can run tomorrow morning, not a plan that expires when a truck is late.

    five numbers — a genome — and you can breed them. Score a population, keep the best, cross the winners,

    Why this is not what AlphaEvolve did
    
    
    
      
    Look where generation 0 startsA population of random genomes, no search at all,
      and the best of them is usually already past candidate #26. That is not the algorithm being clever. Every genome
      in this space is a full-cycle forecasting dispatcher that prices dock queues and chooses between two sorters —
      because a person wrote that structure and left five blanks.
    
      
    A GA can only fill blanksIt cannot add a term. If
      hub_queue were missing from the template, no amount of breeding would
      invent it. The ceiling would simply be lower, and nothing on the chart would tell you why — the population would
      converge just as confidently on a worse answer.
    
      
    AlphaEvolve starts one rung downIts seed is the fixed roster at the top of this page: no
      forecast, no queues, no blanks, 337,402 parcels. It had to write the terms and then price them, in Python, with the
      evaluator as its only feedback. Finding the shape is the hard part; this section is what the easy part looks like
      once you already have it.
